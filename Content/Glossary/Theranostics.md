_Theranostics_ — a blend of "therapy" and "diagnostics" — is the strategy of using the same targeting molecule both to _see_ a disease and to _treat_ it, by swapping what it carries. It is most developed in radiopharmaceuticals, where a single targeting vector (with its [[Chelator|chelator]]) can be loaded either with a gentle _imaging_ radioactive atom or with a damaging _therapeutic_ one.

The pairing works like this. First, the vector is loaded with an imaging isotope — one that emits radiation a scanner can detect from outside the body — and given to the patient. A scan then shows exactly where the drug goes: it confirms the target is actually present on that patient's tumour, reveals every deposit of disease, and shows how much drug reaches the tumour versus healthy organs. If the picture is favourable, the _same_ vector is then loaded with a therapeutic isotope — one emitting short-range, cell-killing radiation — and given to deliver the dose precisely where the scan showed it would land.

This makes theranostics a built-in form of precision medicine: the diagnostic scan _selects the patients_ who will benefit — only those whose disease clearly takes up the drug — and predicts where the therapy will act, before any treatment is given, sparing others a useless or harmful dose. The approach depends on having a matched pair of radioactive atoms — one for imaging, one for therapy — that behave chemically alike, so the imaging version truly predicts where the therapeutic version will go.
