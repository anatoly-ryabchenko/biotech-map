A _ternary complex_ is a structure of three things bound together at once. In protein-degrader drugs the term means the crucial assembly the drug has to form: the _target protein_, the drug, and an [[E3 ligase|E3 ubiquitin ligase]], all held together — the target on one side, the ligase on the other, bridged by the degrader in the middle.

This three-way clasp is the whole point of a degrader, because only when target and ligase are held close, in the right relative position, can the ligase tag the target with ubiquitin and send it for destruction through the [[Ubiquitin-proteasome system|ubiquitin–proteasome system]]. Forming the complex is necessary but not sufficient: the _geometry_ matters intensely. The target has to be presented to the ligase so that an accessible spot on it lines up with the ligase's ubiquitin-transferring machinery; a complex that holds the two proteins in the wrong orientation produces no tagging and no degradation, even though everything is bound.

The best degraders do more than just tether two proteins — they form a _cooperative_ ternary complex, in which the target and ligase also make favourable direct contacts with each other (not only with the drug), which stabilizes the complex and sharpens selectivity for the intended target. This is why degrader design leans so heavily on the linker that joins the drug's two ends: its length and rigidity decide whether a productive, cooperative geometry can form at all.
