An _E3 ubiquitin ligase_ is the enzyme that decides which protein the cell will mark for destruction. It is the selective component of the [[Ubiquitin-proteasome system|ubiquitin–proteasome system]]: where ubiquitin is the "destroy me" tag and the proteasome is the shredder, the E3 ligase is the matchmaker that brings the tag to the right target — holding the doomed protein and the ubiquitin-carrying machinery close enough that the tag is transferred onto it.

Cells have _hundreds_ of different E3 ligases, and each recognizes a particular set of target proteins by specific features — a shape, a chemical modification, a short degradation "address" on the target. This division of labour is what lets a cell destroy exactly the right proteins at the right moment. Most E3 ligases work not alone but as part of larger multi-protein assemblies.

E3 ligases are central to protein-degrader drugs, which work by artificially recruiting one of them to a disease protein it would normally ignore. In practice only a handful are used for this, because the drug needs a good chemical "handle" to grab the ligase — above all _cereblon_ (the ligase the thalidomide-class drugs bind) and _VHL_, for which strong small-molecule binders exist. A PROTAC carries such a handle on one end; a molecular glue instead reshapes the ligase's surface so it grips a new target. Which ligase is chosen matters, because different ligases are present in different amounts in different tissues — which helps decide where a degrader will work.
