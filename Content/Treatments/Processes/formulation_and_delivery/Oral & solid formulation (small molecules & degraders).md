Most small molecules and degraders are meant to be swallowed, so the job here is to turn the optimized compound — a powder — into a pill that survives the shelf, dissolves in the gut, and is absorbed into the blood. A compound that works in a test tube is worthless if the body never takes it up.

The first task is choosing the right _solid form_ of the molecule. The same compound can crystallize into different internal arrangements (_polymorphs_), or be paired with a counter-ion to form a _salt_, and these forms differ enormously in how fast they dissolve and how stable they are; most marketed oral drugs use a salt chosen partly to dissolve better. The form is locked in early, because a late switch to a different crystal can change the drug's behaviour and even derail a program.

The central obstacle is _solubility_, and it is acute for these modalities: a large fraction of modern small molecules barely dissolve in water, and protein degraders are worse — their size pushes them "beyond the rule of five," the rough range within which molecules dissolve and absorb well, almost by construction. When a plain crystal won't dissolve fast enough, more aggressive formulation is used, above all the _amorphous solid dispersion_: the drug is held in a disordered, non-crystalline state inside a polymer (made by spray-drying or melt-extrusion), which can raise apparent solubility a thousand-fold — at the cost of a tendency to creep back to the stable crystal over time, which must be prevented.

The chosen form is built into a tablet or capsule with inactive ingredients — fillers, binders, disintegrants that make it break apart, coatings — tuned so the dose releases on the right schedule. The measure of success is [[Bioavailability|bioavailability]]: the fraction of a swallowed dose that actually reaches the bloodstream intact.

The characteristic difficulty is that solubility and absorption pull against stability and manufacturability — the amorphous form that dissolves best is the least stable, and a drug that survives stomach acid may still not cross the gut wall. When no oral form can deliver enough drug, the program may be forced to an injection instead. The output is a stable, absorbable dose form, ready for testing.
