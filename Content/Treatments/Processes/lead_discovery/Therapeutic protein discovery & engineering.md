This modality's drug is a protein the body already uses, supplied or improved as a medicine: an enzyme to replace one a patient's cells cannot make (enzyme-replacement therapy), a hormone such as insulin, an immune-signaling protein such as an interferon, or a "trap" that soaks up an overactive signal. Unlike an antibody (selected to bind a target) or a designed protein (invented from scratch), the starting sequence is largely known — it is nature's. So the work is selecting the right protein and then engineering it to behave as a drug.

The first decision is which protein and in what form. A straight replacement uses the natural human sequence. More often the natural protein is improved, and the most common move is to fuse it to the _Fc region_ — the stem of an antibody. The body recycles the Fc stem instead of clearing it, so fusing a protein to it can stretch a lifetime in the blood from minutes to days or weeks; the traps etanercept and aflibercept are built this way.

The second, decisive piece of engineering is sugar. Most of these proteins are decorated with branched sugar chains — see [[Glycosylation|glycosylation]] — and those chains are not optional ornament: they control how long the protein survives, whether the immune system tolerates it, and, for replacement enzymes, whether the protein is even taken up by the cells that need it (a lysosomal enzyme must carry a specific sugar tag or it never reaches its destination). Because the sugar pattern is added by the cell that makes the protein, a key choice here is the [[Protein expression|expression system]] — the engineered cells the protein will be grown in — and those cell lines are deliberately altered to attach the right sugars.

The characteristic difficulty is that these proteins are large, fragile, and correct only if folded and decorated exactly right: a sequence perfect on paper can be made with the wrong sugars or fold poorly and fail as a drug, even though it is "the natural protein." The output is a chosen, engineered protein sequence paired with a matched expression system, ready for developability work.
