A vaccine does not treat a disease directly — it teaches the immune system to recognize a threat in advance, so that if the real pathogen (or a tumor) later appears, the body destroys it before it takes hold. The thing the immune system is taught to recognize is an [[Antigen|antigen]]: a piece of the pathogen, usually one of its surface proteins. This step decides exactly which antigen to present, and in what physical form.

Two design problems run together. The first is _which piece_: pinning down the [[Epitope|epitope]], the specific region a protective response must be aimed at. A good antigen shows off the epitopes that provoke antibodies able to actually neutralize the pathogen — not merely antibodies that bind it harmlessly. The second, often decisive, is _shape_. Many viral surface proteins change shape sharply when they infect a cell, springing from a "prefusion" to a "postfusion" form, and the protective epitopes exist only on the prefusion shape. A naïve antigen relaxes into the useless postfusion form, so structure-based design re-engineers the protein to lock it in the prefusion shape, adding stabilizing bonds or amino-acid substitutions. This single insight rescued respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccines after decades of failure and underlies the COVID-19 spike antigens.

The antigen is chosen together with its _platform_ — the form it is delivered in — narrated here as variants: a purified protein grown in cells (a subunit vaccine); a messenger-RNA strand instructing the patient's own cells to make the antigen; a harmless carrier virus delivering the antigen's gene (a viral-vector vaccine); or a whole killed or weakened pathogen. The platform shapes every later step.

The characteristic difficulty is that the immune response is hard to predict from structure alone: an antigen that looks right can raise antibodies that bind but fail to neutralize, or simply too few of them. That is why the next step adds an immune-boosting ingredient, and why this modality folds antigen refinement into discovery rather than running a separate optimization stage. The output is a defined antigen and chosen platform.
