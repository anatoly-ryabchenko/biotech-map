The draft differentiation route from discovery rarely works well on the first try: it yields too few of the wanted cells, a mix of unwanted ones, and cells that are immature or unsafe. This step refines the route into a reliable recipe, run as a test-and-adjust loop.

Several things are pushed at once. _Yield_ — the fraction of starting [[Induced pluripotent stem cell|stem cells]] that reach the target cell type — is raised by tuning which signaling molecules are added, at what dose, in what order, and for how long, since the cells must be walked through the correct developmental stages in sequence. _Purity_ is improved so the batch is mostly the intended cell type, with steps added to remove or select against contaminants. _Maturity_ is pushed too, because cells hurried down a developmental path often stall in a fetal-like, half-finished state that does not behave like the adult cell it is meant to replace. And the whole route is reworked to be _scalable_ — to run in large, controlled vessels rather than hand-tended dishes.

The overriding safety task is eliminating leftover _undifferentiated_ stem cells. Because they keep the power to divide without limit, even a few surviving in the final product can grow into a tumor (a teratoma) after transplant — the signature danger of this modality. So the protocol is engineered both to drive differentiation to completion and to include a way to detect and purge any stragglers.

The characteristic difficulty is that differentiation in a dish only approximates the body's own developmental program, which unfolds with cues and neighbouring cells a culture cannot fully reproduce — so batches drift, maturity plateaus short of the real adult cell, and a protocol that works at small scale can behave differently scaled up. When the route cannot be made to yield safe, mature, pure cells, the work loops back to reconsider the cell source or the route. The output is a robust, scalable differentiation protocol producing safe, functional cells, ready to be formulated.
