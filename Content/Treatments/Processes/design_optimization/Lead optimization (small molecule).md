A hit proves only that some compound can touch the target. Lead optimization turns that rough starting point into a single compound good enough to test in animals — one that binds the target tightly, ignores everything else, and behaves well inside a living body. It is the longest, most chemistry-intensive stretch of early drug discovery, and it runs as a repeating loop.

That loop is the _design–make–test–analyze cycle_. Chemists propose a change to the molecule, synthesize the new version, test it in a battery of assays, read the results, and let them dictate the next change — turning the loop perhaps weekly, dozens to hundreds of times. What guides each turn is the [[Structure-activity relationship|structure–activity relationship]]: the accumulated map of how each change to the molecule's structure shifts its behaviour, so chemists are steering, not guessing.

Several properties are pushed at once, and they fight one another. _Potency_ — how tightly the compound engages the target — is driven up, often from weak (micromolar) to strong (nanomolar) binding. _Selectivity_ is sharpened so the compound hits its target and not its close relatives, the usual source of side effects. And the compound's [[ADME|ADME]] behaviour — how it is absorbed, spread through the body, broken down, and cleared — is tuned so it survives long enough at the right place: a compound the liver destroys in minutes, or that never crosses into cells, is useless however potent. Specific safety traps are screened out early, above all binding to the _hERG_ heart channel, which can cause fatal arrhythmias and has killed many otherwise good drugs.

The characteristic difficulty is that these goals trade against one another: the change that boosts potency often worsens solubility or invites metabolism, so optimization is a constant balancing act across many axes rather than a march toward one number. When a chemical series hits a wall it cannot get past — a liability that cannot be engineered out — the project loops back to hit discovery for a fresh starting scaffold. The output is a single optimized _lead_ compound (with a backup or two), ready to be made into a dose and tested.
