The candidate strains, or draft consortium, from discovery showed promising activity; this step studies each one in depth and selects the final set — with safety foremost, because the drug is a living, self-replicating organism that will take up residence inside a patient.

The cornerstone is reading each strain's complete genome — sequencing all of its DNA — and combing it for anything dangerous: genes for toxins, genes that would let it invade or damage human cells, and especially _antibiotic-resistance genes_. Resistance genes matter doubly, because one could spread from the drug to other bacteria in the patient's gut, and because regulators require a full inventory of them. Resistance is also tested directly, by measuring which antibiotics still kill the strain. Identity is pinned down to the exact strain, not merely the species, since safety and behaviour are strain-specific.

Alongside safety, each strain's _potency_ — the activity that made it a candidate, whether producing a beneficial molecule, blocking a pathogen, or calming inflammation — is measured in defined assays so strains can be ranked. _Manufacturability_ is weighed too: a strain that grows robustly and survives processing is far preferable to a fragile one. For a consortium, the strains are checked for cooperating rather than competing, and the set is often trimmed to the smallest group that still delivers the full function.

The characteristic difficulty is that a strain's genome and its behaviour in a dish only partly predict what it will do inside a living gut: a gene's mere presence does not mean it is switched on, an organism harmless in pure culture may act differently amid a real microbial community, and the safety framework built for single, well-defined chemical drugs fits awkwardly onto a living organism. When a promising strain carries an unacceptable safety flag or cannot be grown reliably, it is dropped and the search loops back. The output is a final, fully characterized strain or defined consortium, ready for preclinical testing of colonization and function.
