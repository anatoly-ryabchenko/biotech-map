A **conjugated biologic** — most often an antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) — is a targeting antibody chemically bolted to a potent cell-killing drug. The antibody acts as a guided delivery vehicle: it homes to a marker on diseased cells (typically a cancer) and carries the toxic _payload_ straight to them, sparing the rest of the body the poison. It combines an antibody's precision with a chemical drug's killing power.

Three parts define it, all carried through the pipeline: the antibody (discovered and engineered just like a plain [[Antibody|antibody]]), the toxic payload, and the _linker_ joining them — which must stay locked in the bloodstream and release the payload only inside the target cell. Its characteristic risk is the payload coming loose where it shouldn't, poisoning healthy tissue, so much of the work is controlling the linker's stability and the number of payloads per antibody. Because its early steps are shared with the antibody modality, a conjugated biologic forks off only once the payload is attached.
