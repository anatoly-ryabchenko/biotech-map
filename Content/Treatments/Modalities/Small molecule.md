A **small molecule** is a compact, chemically synthesized compound — usually under about 500 daltons — small enough to slip inside cells and lodge in a pocket on a target protein, switching its activity off or on. They are the classic drug: most pills are small molecules. Their advantages are that they can be taken by mouth, reach targets inside cells (where larger drugs cannot go), are comparatively cheap to manufacture at scale, and clear from the body on a convenient timescale. Their main limit is that they need a well-formed pocket to bind, so targets with flat, featureless surfaces are hard to act on this way.

Mechanistically, a small molecule works by binding its target and either _inhibiting_ it (blocking the pocket the target needs to function) or _modulating_ it (changing its shape or activity). The whole pipeline is built around finding a compound that binds tightly and selectively, behaves well in the body, and can be made into a stable pill.
