A **de novo** protein is a brand-new protein designed from scratch on a computer, shaped specifically to act on a target — and not on anything else. Unlike a [[Therapeutic protein|therapeutic protein]] (whose sequence comes from nature) or an [[Antibody|antibody]] (selected from a vast library of natural-like candidates), a de novo protein has no natural template: its shape and sequence are invented to do a particular job, whether simply blocking a target, switching its shape, or even catalysing a reaction as a designed enzyme.

The work begins almost entirely [[In silico|in silico]] — generating a shape, finding a sequence that will fold into it, and checking the design by prediction — because building and testing real proteins is slow and expensive, so the idea is to get the design as right as possible on the screen first. Because today's design tools are trained largely on natural proteins, genuinely novel designs sit where the models are least reliable, so the modality is highly iterative, leaning hard on a wet-lab validation step that confirms which computer designs actually fold and work. Past discovery and validation it joins the shared protein-biologics pipeline.
