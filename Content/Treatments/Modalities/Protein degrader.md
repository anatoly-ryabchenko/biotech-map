A **protein degrader** is a small molecule that does not block its target but destroys it. It hijacks the cell's own waste-disposal system — the [[Ubiquitin-proteasome system|ubiquitin–proteasome system]] — to tag a chosen disease protein for shredding. Because a degrader only needs to touch its target briefly to trigger destruction, rather than continuously occupy a functional pocket, it can act on targets that have no good pocket at all — many proteins long considered "undruggable" by ordinary small molecules.

The modality has two forms, both narrated through the pipeline: _PROTACs_, two-headed molecules that physically bridge the target and one of the cell's [[E3 ligase|E3 ligases]], and _molecular glues_, smaller molecules that reshape an E3 ligase so it grips a new target. Degraders work catalytically — one molecule can trigger the destruction of many target molecules — but their large size makes getting them into cells and dosing them orally a central challenge.
