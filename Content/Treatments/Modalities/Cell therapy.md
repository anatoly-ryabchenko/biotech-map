A **cell therapy** turns living cells — usually a patient's own T cells, the immune system's killers — into a drug by engineering them with a new receptor that makes them recognize and destroy diseased cells, most often a cancer. The medicine is not a molecule but a population of living, engineered cells that, once infused, multiply and act inside the body.

The engineered receptor is a [[Chimeric antigen receptor|chimeric antigen receptor]] (CAR) or an engineered T-cell receptor; the pipeline designs it, builds it into real cells (delivering the gene with a [[Lentiviral vector|lentiviral vector]]), and grows the cells into a dose. Because most cell therapies are _autologous_ — made from each patient's own cells — manufacturing is a personalized, one-batch-per-patient effort with no second chance. The defining risks are choosing a target truly confined to diseased cells (since the cells kill everything bearing it) and the violent immune reactions the living cells can trigger. As a permanent, living therapy it carries long-term follow-up and a specialized regulatory route.
