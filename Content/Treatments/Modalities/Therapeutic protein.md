A **therapeutic protein** is a protein the body already uses, supplied or improved as a medicine: an enzyme to replace one a patient's cells cannot make (enzyme-replacement therapy), a hormone such as insulin, an immune-signalling protein such as an interferon, or a "trap" that mops up an overactive signal. It is distinct from an [[Antibody|antibody]] (which is selected to _bind_ a target) and from a [[De novo|designed protein]] (invented from scratch): here the starting sequence is largely nature's, so the work is selecting the right protein and engineering it to behave as a drug.

These proteins are grown in living cells and given by injection. The key engineering levers are fusing the protein to an antibody stem (an _Fc-fusion_) to make it last far longer in the blood, and controlling its [[Glycosylation|sugar coating]], which governs its stability, immune tolerance, and — for replacement enzymes — whether the right cells even take it up. Beyond one dedicated discovery step, the modality rides the same protein-biologics machinery as antibodies and designed proteins.
