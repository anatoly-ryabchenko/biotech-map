An **oncolytic virus** is a virus engineered to infect and burst cancer cells while sparing healthy ones — and, just as importantly, to alert the immune system to the tumour as it does so. It is two things at once: a self-amplifying drug, since each infected cell that bursts releases a fresh wave of virus, and an immune trigger that can rouse a body-wide attack on the cancer.

The pipeline starts from a chosen viral backbone (such as a herpes or adeno virus) and engineers it on two fronts carried throughout: _tumour selectivity_, by deleting viral genes the virus needs only in healthy cells, so it can thrive only where a cancer's broken defenses supply those functions; and _arming_, by inserting a gene (often the immune-recruiting signal GM-CSF) that turns the infected tumour into a beacon for the immune system. Its defining tensions are keeping the virus weak enough to be safe yet strong enough to spread and kill, and reaching tumours at all — today it is mostly injected directly into accessible tumours. As a live, replicating therapy it carries long-term follow-up and a specialized regulatory route.
