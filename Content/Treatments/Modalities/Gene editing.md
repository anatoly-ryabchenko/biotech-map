A **gene editing** therapy repairs the gene already in the patient's cells, rewriting their own DNA at one exact spot — where a [[Gene therapy|gene therapy]] adds a separate working copy, editing fixes the original. The tool is [[CRISPR|CRISPR]]: a protein that cuts or chemically alters DNA, steered to a chosen site by a short guide RNA whose letters match that site.

The modality spans three levels of precision, carried through the pipeline as variants: a clean cut that disables a harmful gene; _base editing_, which rewrites a single DNA letter without cutting; and _prime editing_, which pastes in a short newly written sequence. A further split is whether the editing is done _inside_ the body or on cells removed, edited, and returned. The defining concern — and the focus of much of the pipeline — is that any edit made at an unintended site is permanent and potentially dangerous, so the work centres on proving the edit lands only where meant. Like other genome-altering therapies it carries long-term follow-up and a specialized regulatory route.
