A **radiopharmaceutical** destroys diseased cells by delivering radiation to them from the inside. It has three parts bolted together: a _targeting vector_ that homes to a marker on the diseased cells, a radioactive atom that does the killing, and a [[Chelator|chelator]] — a cage-like molecule that grips the radioactive metal and ties it to the vector.

A defining feature of the modality is the [[Theranostics|theranostic]] pairing: the same vector and chelator can carry either a gentle imaging isotope, whose scan confirms the target is present and selects which patients will benefit, or a therapeutic isotope that then treats those patients. The pipeline turns on getting the radiation to land only where intended — a vector that lingers or a chelator that leaks irradiates healthy organs — and on a relentless clock, since the radioactive atom decays from the moment it is made, forcing manufacturing and dosing into a tight window. Because it is radioactive, it passes through an extra regulatory layer governing radioactive-materials handling, on top of the usual drug approval.
