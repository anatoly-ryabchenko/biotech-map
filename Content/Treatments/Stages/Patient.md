Patient is the stage the whole pipeline has been building toward: **getting the right therapy into the right patient safely, and then watching it over time in the real world.** Everything before — finding the target, designing the medicine, proving it works and is safe, getting it approved, and making it — exists to reach this moment.

How the therapy is given depends on what it is: a pill swallowed at home, an injection or a slow infusion, a gene therapy delivered into the blood or an organ, a virus injected straight into a tumor, a radioactive drug after which the patient briefly emits radiation, a simple vaccine shot, a swallowed microbiome medicine. The care setting matches the risk — a pill needs little supervision, while a cell therapy or a strong antibody infusion can trigger dangerous reactions and so is given under close hospital monitoring. For many targeted medicines a companion test first confirms the patient's disease actually carries the target, so the right drug reaches the right person. Beyond the moment of treatment, this stage also covers the ongoing real-world work after launch: watching the medicine's safety in a far larger and more varied population than any trial could enroll, and keeping it reliably manufactured and supplied for years.

This is where treatment is actually delivered and then watched; there is no later stage to advance to. The optional post-approval surveillance extends beyond the delivered treatment and, when real-world evidence reveals a serious new risk, loops back to large confirmatory testing rather than moving forward.
