Manufacturing answers the question: **can we make this approved medicine reliably, purely, and identically at the scale needed to supply every patient?** Making a small amount of something in a research lab and making a great deal of it consistently are very different challenges, and this stage is about the second one — scaling production up to commercial volumes under a binding quality system that requires every step documented, every material tested, and every batch traceable.

The work depends on what the medicine is. A small molecule's chemical route is often redesigned for large equipment — fewer, safer steps, with impurities identified and driven below strict limits — and then run at scale, where reactions behave differently than in a flask. Antibodies and designed proteins are grown by living cells in large, tightly controlled tanks and then purified out of everything else. Each of the other modalities — peptides, conjugates, mRNA, oligonucleotides, viral vectors, editing components, cell products, oncolytic viruses, radiopharmaceuticals, microbiome products, vaccines — has its own specialized production and release process. The common thread is consistency: any batch must be as safe and effective as any other, proven by testing every batch and validating that the process reliably makes a product within specification.

**A deliberate simplification.** This stage models the **commercial-scale technical scale-up** — making the approved product reliably and in quantity. That is a simplification of how the industry really works: medicine is in fact made much earlier and at smaller scale, because the material used in the clinical trials themselves has to be produced to the same quality standards, and process-development work runs in parallel throughout development. This stage deliberately models only the scale-up step; it is **not** about commercialization, marketing, or selling the drug.

The stage is done when there is a **validated, scalable production process making the drug reliably at commercial scale**, ready to be delivered to patients.
